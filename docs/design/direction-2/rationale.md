# Direction 2: Keynote Stage

**The thesis in one sentence:** deep navy is the ground the way a darkened auditorium is the ground for a lit stage, and every piece of content arrives as a pool of light: bone paper, duotoned photography, and a headline scaled like it is being projected on the back wall.

## Why this is right for this person and this audience

Damian sells one thing: 60 to 90 minutes in front of a room. The buyer is a meeting planner at a farm credit association or a commodity board, and what she needs to believe in three seconds is that this man is comfortable on a big stage in front of her people. A site that opens on white with a hero photo and three feature cards is a brochure. A site that opens on a dark room with a 120px headline is the room she is trying to fill.

The stage frame also solves a content problem. His copy is blunt and short: "Straight-Forward Agriculture Dialogue" is three words doing all the work. Copy that short dies inside a 16px template. Given 120px of Archivo it becomes the marquee it was always meant to be. Scale is the argument, not decoration.

Restraint carries the rest. One orange action per viewport, one 3px orange rule under each eyebrow, nothing else competing. The proof row is four numbers on hairlines: no icons, no cards, no gradient tiles. That reads as expensive to the same people who read a clean annual report as expensive.

## Making the fixed wordmark look deliberate

The wordmark sets "BUSINESS · AGRICULTURE" between two orange rules under navy type. The system quotes that structure back. The masthead is a bone paper strip with a 3px orange rule at its base, so the logo sits on its native paper and the header repeats the logo's own device at page scale. Every eyebrow is short tracked type over a short orange rule: the lockup, rebuilt as layout.

The navy is handled the same way. #094D78 is not the page background. It is one rung on a nine-step ramp built from its own hue, and the page sits deeper at #041B2C. The logo holds its exact value while the site goes darker than the logo could, which is why the mark reads as chosen rather than tolerated. Orange gets a darker step (#A62E0E) for small text on bone, so it passes contrast without anyone repainting the brand.

## The type pairing

Archivo for display, IBM Plex Sans for body, Newsreader for pull quotes and named claims, IBM Plex Mono for labels and numerals. Archivo is a tight American grotesk that gets denser and more certain at large sizes, where Inter and its relatives go limp. Plex Sans is humanist, so it does not twin with Archivo the way a second neo-grotesque would. Newsreader is the direction's insurance policy: it is why "Thought Provoking / Engaging / Enthusiastic / Hilarious" reads as an editorial list rather than a feature grid, and why a navy page with a giant grotesk headline does not end up feeling like a Series B software company. Mono keeps the stats reading like a run-of-show cue sheet.

## Handling weak photography

No slot depends on a good photo. Photography enters as one duotone: grayscale, blended in luminosity over navy, then scrimmed with a directional gradient. The hero shot has a green and blue stage wash and a branded lectern in it, and duotoning turns that clash into art direction. The testimonial photo runs at 50 percent opacity behind the quote, so it is the room, not the subject. Client logos get the same logic: one optical box, grayscale, multiply on bone, so 20 mismatched files read as one wall. If a great photo arrives later, raising two opacity tokens is the whole upgrade.

## The biggest risk, honestly

A page that is dark for 70 percent of its height can tip from "auditorium" into "enterprise SaaS." If the bone sections get compressed in build, or the Newsreader accents get value-engineered out, this direction loses its warmth and becomes a competent, cold, forgettable dark site. The bone and the serif are load-bearing, not garnish.
