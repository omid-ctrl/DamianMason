# Direction 3 — Ledger and Land

**Thesis: Damian Mason reads the numbers and works the ground, so the site is set like a well-kept financial table printed on good paper: warm bone stock, deep navy ground, hairline rules doing structural work, and every proof point typeset as a figure in a ledger column rather than a claim in a marketing sentence.**

## Why this, for this man and this room

The people who write the check are farm credit executives, commodity board directors, and association staff. They spend their working lives inside spreadsheets and board packets. A ledger is their native visual language, and nothing reads as more credible to them than a number set correctly in a column with a rule above it.

So the stat row is not a "stat row." It is a table: a heavy navy rule, a hairline beneath it, four ruled cells with mono row indices, tabular lining figures, and a monospace footnote carrying the verbatim source sentence. Damian's voice guide says hard numbers do the persuading and that he "almost never reaches for an intensifier when a figure is available." The design agrees. 2,400 and 40,000 are the largest type on the page after the masthead, and the only thing colored hot orange is the `+` on each, because the `+` is the part actually doing work.

The same logic runs everywhere: the open dropdown is a ruled index, the four checklist claims become numbered rows, the client wall is a hairline grid with a cell number in every corner, and the video testimonials become a four-row register. One idea, applied to everything. That is what stops a rebuild from reading as a theme.

## How the fixed wordmark stops looking tolerated

The palette is a straight tonal extension of the wordmark navy (nine steps out of `#094D78`) laid on warm bone, so the logo's blue sits inside a family instead of on top of a stranger. The orange is handled by rationing rather than matching: five jobs on the whole page and no others (primary button, the `+` on two figures, the pull-quote rule, the open-nav marker, the quotation marks). The important one is the button, a solid `#FF5325` field with navy type on it. That button is literally the wordmark's two colors doing a job, which makes the wordmark read as the source of the system rather than a legacy asset worked around. Harvest gold and deep field green carry the secondary structural load, which keeps orange scarce enough to stay hot.

## The type pairing

**Source Serif 4** for display, **Archivo** for body, **IBM Plex Mono** for ledger keys. The serif is humanist and warm in its terminals, but its axis is upright and businesslike, so an 80px masthead reads as an annual report set by someone with taste rather than as a revival. It carries tabular lining figures, which is why the ledger holds its columns. Archivo is deliberately flatter: the serif carries voice, the grotesk carries information, and a 55-year-old meeting planner can read the FAQ on a laptop. Plex Mono, used only for indices, eyebrows, captions, and footnotes, is the typographic sound of a ruled column.

## Weak photography

Two token-driven duotones. Portraits get navy shadows and bone highlights; event photography gets navy shadows and pale gold highlights with a brightness lift. The stage shot has garish blue and green uplighting and a podium in frame; the duotone kills the color problem outright, and a hard 21:8 crop with a mono caption turns a snapshot into a plate. Every photo slot is optional: remove both images and the layout still holds, because the type and the rules are the structure.

## The honest risk

Restraint this severe can tip into austerity: an all-bone page of hairlines and small monospace could read as cold or bureaucratic to an audience that also comes to Damian to laugh, and if a future editor adds sections without adding warmth, the site could end up looking more like a bank's annual report than like a man who studied at Second City.
