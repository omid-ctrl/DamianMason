# Direction 1: Editorial Broadsheet

## The thesis, in one sentence

Build the site as a printed broadsheet: bone paper is the ground, navy is ink rather than background, and a
visible editorial grid of hairline rules, section numbers, running heads, and a high-contrast Didone does the
work that a stock theme would hand to gradients and drop shadows.

## Why this is right for this man and this room

Damian Mason is an agricultural economist who talks in numbers. His voice guide measures it: figures do the
persuading, never intensifiers. A broadsheet is the layout system that treats a number as a display element.
The stat row is not a widget with an icon, it is the paper's record: 2,400+, 50, 1994, 40,000+, each in a
ruled cell with a mono label under it, the way a market table runs.

The audience is farm credit executives, commodity boards, and the meeting planners who book them. They read
long documents for a living and they won't scroll past three screens of atmosphere to reach a fee question. So
this direction is deliberately dense. The hero carries an index of credentials, programs, and home office
before the fold ends. The copy section runs the real paragraph in two true columns with a drop cap instead of
chopping it into cards. Density reads as competence here, not clutter, because the format is familiar.

It also solves the "designed for one person" problem structurally. Section numbers, folios, and a running head
reading "The Business of Food, Fuel, and Fiber" are furniture you cannot buy on ThemeForest.

## How the fixed wordmark becomes deliberate

The wordmark is navy type with an orange rule under it. This direction turns that relationship into the whole
system. Navy is promoted from brand color to ink: headlines, body copy, rules, and the one dark band. Orange
is demoted from decoration to editorial mark and appears at exactly three places: the kicker bar above the H1,
the drop cap and pull-quote rule, and the podcast figure in the navy band. Each is a rule or a single glyph,
which is exactly what the orange does in the logo. The wordmark stops looking like an artifact the site
tolerates and becomes the smallest instance of the page's own grammar. The warm bone ground (`#F2EBDE`) helps
too: on pure white the brand navy reads cold and the orange reads cheap. On bone, both sit like ink on stock.

## The type pairing

Bodoni Moda for display, Archivo for body, IBM Plex Mono for the machinery. Bodoni Moda is a Didone with a real
optical-size axis, so the 88px headline gets true hairlines and the 20px pull quote stays sturdy. Didones are
the historical headline voice of newspapers, which is the whole argument. Archivo is a grotesque drawn for
editorial density: a large x-height that carries a 17px economist paragraph in two narrow columns without
fatigue, and flat terminals that stop the page going soft next to the serif. Plex Mono sells the concept.
Fixed-width caps at 0.18em tracking make section numbers, captions, and stat labels look typeset, not styled.

## Carrying weak photography

Every image runs one plate treatment: full grayscale, a navy multiply veil at 62 percent, a 10 percent warm
screen. A mediocre convention-hall snapshot becomes a duotone press photo, and the library matches whether it
came from a phone or a photographer. Crops are hard rectangles with no rounding, the plate stretches to the
height of the text column beside it, and a mono caption under a hairline rule does more persuading than the
image does. Logos get the same discipline: grayscale, contrast, and `mix-blend-mode: multiply`, which drops the
white boxes out of the JPEG files so 21 logos of different provenance read as one ruled table.

## The single biggest risk, stated honestly

It can read as cold. This is a man whose defining trait is that he is funny, and a Didone-and-hairline
broadsheet is the most serious register available. If the photography stays this restrained, a meeting planner
could come away thinking "credible economist" and never get to "Second City." The mitigation is to let the
comedy live in the copy and in one warm photo per page at full size, but the risk is real.
